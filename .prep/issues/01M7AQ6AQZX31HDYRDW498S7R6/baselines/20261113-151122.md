Make the app great to use on a phone, both in the browser and as an installed PWA (the user reads mostly on desktop and phone; read state syncs). Goal: one-handed, fast, gesture-friendly reading where every gesture also has a visible, accessible alternative. Research and the current-state gap analysis are recorded in the findings 'Mobile UX research: reader gesture and navigation patterns' and 'Edge-swipe navigation in installed PWAs' (prep knowledge find mobile).

Decided (see the decision records): in the reader, swipe left = next article and swipe right = previous; the hamburger drawer stays (mobile navigation redesign such as a bottom tab bar is deferred); row swipe right = toggle read and left = toggle star; long-press/overflow menu; mark-all-read confirms when it affects many items and always offers Undo; mark-as-read-on-scroll is not built now; the system edge-swipe back gestures cannot be disabled, so our gestures ignore edge starts and Back always returns to the list.

Principles: gestures are shortcuts, never the only way; consistent gestures; touch targets at least 44 px with spacing; respect safe areas and dvh; no accidental destructive actions; keep the existing optimistic state, narrow invalidation and list-persistence architecture (prep knowledge find views); no new heavy dependencies unless justified.

Sub-issues, sequenced by dependency: foundations; swipe between articles; reader action bar; list row gestures; pull to refresh; reader polish; transitions; mobile test harness.
